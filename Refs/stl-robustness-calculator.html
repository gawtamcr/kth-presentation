One trajectory, scored end to end

Eight states, one requirement: ◇[0,3]A ∧ ◇[4,7]B ∧ □[0,7]¬O — reach A in the first four steps, then reach B in the last four, while never sitting inside the obstacle O. Drag a state and every number below it updates.

traj

The trajectory

A one-dimensional position x, one value per timestep t=0…7. The bands are the regions: teal is A, blue is B, orange is the obstacle O. Because the trajectory is a discrete sequence of states, it can skip over O entirely between two timesteps without ever being penalized for it — nothing requires it to visit the states in between.

flow

Scored while it is still forming — not after

The score above is not computed once, on the finished trajectory. It is computed at every step of generation, on the sampler's predicted endpoint τ̂(s) — its running guess of the finished trajectory, re-estimated as flow time s goes from 0 (pure noise) to 1 (the trajectory you built above). Every row in between is one real step of the sampler: a score and a gradient evaluated on a trajectory that is still only a rough guess, which is exactly why the thesis evaluates the requirement at τ̂(s) — an extrapolated, already-plausible-looking estimate — rather than at the raw noisy iterate.
